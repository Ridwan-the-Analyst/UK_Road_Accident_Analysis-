# 📑 Insights Report: UK Road Accident Analysis

## 1. Executive Summary
This project analyzes road traffic accidents in the United Kingdom to understand the patterns, identify risk factors, and recommend actionable improvements.  
Data was visualized through a Power BI dashboard and analyzed through key dimensions such as casualty severity, time, location, light conditions, and vehicle types.

---

## 2. Key Findings

### 📈 Accident Trends
- **Total accidents** recorded: **144,419** (a decrease of **-11.7%** compared to last year).
- Casualties show a similar declining trend (**-11.9%**).

### 🚗 Vehicle Type Analysis
- **Cars** were involved in **80%+** of accidents (155,804 casualties).
- Bikes and Vans also accounted for significant portions (15,610 and 15,905 respectively).

### 🕰️ Accident Timing
- Accidents peaked in **November and December**, possibly due to winter weather conditions and holiday travel.
- Lowest accident rates were observed in the early months (January to March).

### 🔦 Light Conditions
- **Daylight** accidents made up **73.84%** of incidents.
- **Dark conditions** contributed to **26.16%** of accidents.

### 🛣️ Road Type
- **Single carriageways** saw the most casualties (~145K), indicating a need for improved road infrastructure or regulations.

### 🌍 Location
- Higher accident densities in major cities like London, Manchester, and Birmingham.

---

## 3. Recommendations

### 🎯 Safety Campaigns
- Focus on October–December with winter safety tips and awareness campaigns.

### 🚦 Vehicle and Driver Interventions
- Special programs targeting **car drivers** — encourage safer practices.
- Promote **bike safety** by investing in bike lanes and awareness for motorists.

### 🛣️ Infrastructure Improvements
- Install better road markings and dividers on single carriageways.
- Traffic calming measures in urban areas (speed bumps, roundabouts).

### 👁️ Daytime Safety Awareness
- Defensive driving workshops emphasizing focus even during daytime when drivers may be less cautious.

# 📢 Conclusion
With targeted interventions based on data-driven insights, accident rates in the UK can be further reduced, particularly in high-risk periods and areas.
